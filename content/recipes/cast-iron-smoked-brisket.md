## Method

Trim the flat to about 1/4 inch fat cap. Coat with yellow mustard as a binder, then apply the rub: 2:1 pepper to salt ratio, plus garlic powder. Let it sit uncovered in the fridge overnight if you can — otherwise, at least an hour at room temp.

Set the smoker to 225°F with post oak. Place the brisket fat side up and leave it alone for 3 hours. After that, spritz with apple cider vinegar every 45 minutes.

When the bark is set and the internal temp hits about 165°F (usually around hour 5-6), wrap in butcher paper. Continue cooking until the probe slides in like butter — around 200-203°F internal.

Here's where the cast iron comes in: unwrap the brisket, heat the skillet to medium with beef tallow, and sear the bark side down for about 90 seconds. This tightens up any bark that softened during the wrap and builds a fond in the pan. Spoon that fond back over the top.

Rest for at least 1 hour (2 is better) in a cooler wrapped in towels. Slice against the grain, about pencil-width thickness.

## Notes

The cast iron finish is optional but it makes a real difference on flat cuts where the bark can get soft in the wrap. If you're doing a full packer, the point will have enough fat to keep the bark intact — skip the sear on those.
